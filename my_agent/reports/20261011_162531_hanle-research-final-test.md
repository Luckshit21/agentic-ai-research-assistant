**Hanle Research Final Test**

**Summary and Key Findings**
The Hanle Dark Sky Reserve (HDSR), located in eastern Ladakh, is India’s first Dark Sky Reserve. It was established to protect the region’s exceptionally dark night skies, which are critical for astronomical research and astrophotography. The initiative operates as a science-driven socio-economic project with two primary pillars: mitigating light pollution and promoting astro-tourism to benefit local communities.

**Verified Facts**
*   **Notification Date:** The HDSR was officially notified by the Government of Ladakh in December 2022.
*   **Core Facility:** The reserve is centered around the Indian Astronomical Observatory (IAO) at Hanle.
*   **Operational Authority:** The IAO is operated by the Indian Institute of Astrophysics (IIA), an autonomous institution under the Department of Science and Technology (DST).
*   **Objective:** It serves as a destination for astrophotographers and amateur astronomers, driven by the region's superior sky conditions.
*   **Development:** The initiative is supported by stakeholders, including the UT Ladakh government, which funds the astro-tourism and light management plans.

**Uncertainties**
While the IIAP source mentions a specific radius of roughly 22 km for the region, the verified PIB source does not confirm exact geographical dimensions or specific financial figures.
